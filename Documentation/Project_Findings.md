# Project Findings

## 1. Overview

This project analyses road accident data for Delhi (UT) over five years, from 2019 to 2023. The analysis focuses on identifying yearly trends, monthly accident patterns, and time slots with higher and lower accident counts.

The analysis is based on data obtained from the National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB).

A total of **25,783 road accidents** were recorded across the five-year period covered by this analysis.

## 2. Year-wise Accident Trends

The annual accident totals show how the number of reported road accidents changed between 2019 and 2023.

| Year | Total Accidents | Year-on-Year Change |
|------|-----------------|---------------------|
| 2019 | 5,601           |         ---         |
| 2020 | 4,187           |       -25.25%       |
| 2021 | 4,720           |       +12.73%       |
| 2022 | 5,560           |       +17.80%       |
| 2023 | 5,715           |       +2.79%        |

### Key observations

- The highest annual accident count was recorded in **2023**, with 5,715 accidents.
- The lowest annual accident count was recorded in **2020**, with 4,187 accidents.
- Accident counts decreased by approximately 25.25% in 2020 compared with 2019.
- From 2021 onwards, annual accident counts increased each year.
- By 2023, the annual count had risen above the 2019 level.

## 3. Monthly Accident Patterns

The monthly analysis examines how road accident counts were distributed across the twelve months of each year from 2019 to 2023. It helps identify the months that recorded the highest and lowest accident counts in each year.

### Highest and Lowest Accident Months by Year

| Year | Highest Accident Month | Accidents | Lowest Accident Month | Accidents |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2019 | January | 524 | August | 412 |
| 2020 | January | 492 | April | 99 |
| 2021 | December | 485 | May | 243 |
| 2022 | April | 516 | September | 414 |
| 2023 | October | 548 | March | 425 |

### Key Observations

- January recorded the highest monthly accident count in both 2019 and 2020.
- The highest accident count occurred in December in 2021, April in 2022, and October in 2023.
- The lowest accident count varied across the five years, occurring in August, April, May, September, and March respectively.
- October 2023 recorded the highest monthly accident count in this year-wise comparison, with 548 accidents.
- The monthly patterns indicate that the months with higher and lower accident counts were not consistent across all five years.

**Overall, the monthly analysis shows variation in accident counts across different months and years.** Therefore, examining multiple years is useful for understanding how monthly accident patterns change over time.

The time-slot analysis examines the distribution of road accidents across different time periods of the day from 2019 to 2023. It helps identify the time slots with the highest and lowest accident counts in each year.

## 5. Overall Key Findings

The analysis of Delhi road accident data from 2019 to 2023 produced the following key findings:

1. **Overall accident count:** A total of 25,783 road accidents were recorded during the five-year period.

2. **Yearly trend:** The lowest annual accident count was recorded in 2020, with 4,187 accidents, while the highest was recorded in 2023, with 5,715 accidents.

3. **Change after 2020:** Annual accident counts increased every year from 2021 to 2023, following the decrease recorded in 2020.

4. **Monthly variation:** The months with the highest and lowest accident counts varied across the five years. No single month consistently recorded the highest or lowest count.

5. **Evening and late-night patterns:** The highest accident counts in every year were recorded in either the 18:00–21:00 or 21:00–24:00 time slot.

6. **Consistent early-morning pattern:** The 03:00–06:00 time slot recorded the lowest accident count in all five years.

7. **Year-to-year differences:** Both monthly and time-slot distributions changed across the study period, highlighting the importance of analysing accident data over multiple years rather than relying on a single year's results.

These findings provide a descriptive overview of road accident patterns in Delhi and help identify variations across years, months, and time periods.

## 6. Limitations of the Analysis

Although this project provides useful insights into road accident patterns in Delhi between 2019 and 2023, there are some limitations to consider:

1. **Limited study period:** The analysis covers only five years (2019–2023). A longer period could provide a better understanding of long-term trends and changes.

2. **Limited variables:** The analysis focuses on accident counts by year, month, and time slot. It does not examine other factors such as weather conditions, road conditions, vehicle types, or driver behaviour.

3. **No geographical breakdown:** The analysis covers Delhi (UT) as a whole and does not identify accident-prone locations or compare accident counts across individual districts or areas.

4. **No causal analysis:** The project identifies patterns and variations in accident counts but does not establish the reasons behind them. For example, higher accident counts during evening or late-night hours do not prove that those time periods cause more accidents.

5. **Dependence on reported data:** The analysis relies on accident figures published by the National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB). It therefore reflects the available reported data and may not capture incidents that were not recorded in the source data.

Despite these limitations, the project provides a structured overview of road accident patterns in Delhi and demonstrates how data cleaning, SQL analysis, and Power BI visualisation can be used to explore and communicate findings.
